Rainwater platforms & wayfinding markers

Eagle Scout project, Troop 838, Dallas

I planned, designed, and led a community build. I modeled the rainwater collection platforms in CAD, sourced the lumber and hardware, and ran build days with more than 25 volunteers. We then installed the platforms and a set of marker posts on site.

Plan in CAD first

The site needed level, sturdy bases for rain barrels on uneven ground, plus marker posts to help people find their way. Before buying anything, I modeled the platform frames in CAD so I could size the lumber, check how the barrels fit, and create a cut list. This kept costs within the $2,100+ budget.

A full water barrel is heavy, so the platforms needed short spans, closely spaced deck boards, and a frame built to stay level as the ground settled.

Leading 25+ volunteers

I split the work into stations so volunteers of different ages and skill levels could work safely at the same time. Frames were pre-assembled in a workshop, then carried to the site to be leveled and installed. I handled purchasing, scheduling, tool safety briefings, and quality checks, including checking level on every platform before it was finished.

  • Materials: pressure-treated lumber, bought and transported in batches
  • Workshop: frames laid out and assembled in parallel
  • On site: ground prepared, frames leveled, decks and railings attached
  • Markers: stenciled posts with solar caps, set in the ground at each location
