Torsional rigidity test jig

Texas A&M Formula SAE EV, chassis subteam · AME26 chassis

As an apprentice on the Texas A&M Formula SAE EV chassis subteam, I designed a test jig that measures how stiff the AME26 chassis is in torsion, so the team can check its FEA against the real frame. The back of the chassis is anchored into concrete. The front sits on a center pivot with long lever arms, so a weight hung on one side twists the chassis with a known torque. I designed it from scratch in SolidWorks, and it’s about to be manufactured.

Why test it

Torsional stiffness is how much torque it takes to twist one end of the chassis by one degree relative to the other. It’s one of the main numbers a Formula SAE chassis is designed around, and it’s usually predicted with FEA.

An FEA result is only as good as its model: how the joints are represented, the tube properties, and above all the boundary conditions. Twisting the real frame by a known amount is the way to find out whether the model can be trusted.

How the jig works

  • The rear is held fixed. A welded steel fixture is anchored into concrete. Its mock uprights, made from water-jetted and welded steel, stand where the rear wheel uprights would be, and the suspension is replaced with rigid parts so the back of the chassis can’t move.
  • The front can only roll. The front beam rests on a V-shaped pivot at the car’s centerline. Mock uprights on the beam hold the front of the chassis the same way the rear ones do.
  • Weights apply a known torque. The beam extends past the mock uprights to a post at each end, 1.15 m from the pivot. A weight hung on one post twists the front of the chassis about the pivot with a torque of weight × lever arm.
  • Twist gives the stiffness. Comparing the applied torque with how far the front rotates relative to the fixed rear gives the torsional stiffness, K = T / θ, which can be compared directly with the FEA prediction.

Status and next steps

We were assigned the jig in mid-September. The design is finished and it’s about to be manufactured. Once it’s built, the chassis goes on the jig and the measured stiffness gets compared with the FEA prediction. I’ll add the results here when we have them.

This was also my first project in SolidWorks. Before this, I’d used Onshape and Fusion 360.
